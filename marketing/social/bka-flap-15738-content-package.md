# How One Flap Can More Than Double the WRVUs of a Below-Knee Amputation

## LinkedIn Version

Most surgeons think of a below-knee amputation as one operation.

Sometimes it is.

Sometimes the real work is building a residual limb the patient can actually walk on.

Standard BKA:
CPT 27880 = 14.99 wRVUs

BKA + lower extremity flap reconstruction:
CPT 27880 + 15738 = 33.55 wRVUs

That is a 123.8% increase in physician work RVUs.

The key point: this is surgery, not coding.

Formal flap reconstruction, myodesis, soft tissue advancement, and durable bone coverage require real technical work. If that work is performed, the operative note should clearly describe it.

Not aggressive billing. Accurate documentation.

The hardest part is not always taking the leg off. It is building a limb the patient can actually walk on.

## Email Newsletter Version

Subject: The BKA documentation miss that can hide half the work

Most surgeons know CPT 27880 for below-knee amputation.

Fewer surgeons stop and document the reconstructive work when a BKA requires formal lower extremity flap reconstruction, myodesis, and durable residual limb creation.

Here is the clean teaching example:

- Below-knee amputation, CPT 27880: 14.99 wRVUs
- Lower extremity flap reconstruction, CPT 15738: 18.56 wRVUs
- Combined educational total: 33.55 wRVUs

That is a 123.8% increase in physician work RVUs.

The flap contributes more physician work than the amputation itself.

This is not about aggressive billing. It is about accurately documenting the reconstructive work actually performed. A note that says "flaps were fashioned and secured" does not describe tissue mobilization, flap design, myodesis, bone coverage, or the construction of a prosthetic-ready residual limb.

The difference is surgery, not coding.
